Research

My group develops AI methods for scientific discovery, with a focus on agentic AI systems that act as virtual bioinformaticians, computational biology methods that uncover gene regulation and the genetic basis of complex diseases such as Alzheimer's disease, and trustworthy AI that keeps large language models secure against jailbreaks and prompt injection. A recurring theme is that knowledge learned from data should hold beyond a single dataset or experimental protocol.

I am always happy to talk with motivated students interested in these topics, about either admission or remote research collaborations. Feel free to reach out.

Agentic AI for Science

Multi-agent LLM systems that automate complex, real-world data analysis, especially in AI for science.

  • Virtual bioinformaticians for genomics (GenoMAS, GenoTEX)
  • Statistical understanding and efficiency of multi-agent systems and test-time scaling

Computational Biology

We study computational biology because every advance has the potential to free millions from suffering.

  • Gene regulatory structure from spatial and single-cell transcriptomics
  • Genetic basis of complex traits, especially Alzheimer's disease and cancer

Trustworthy & Secure AI

Defending LLM-driven AI against dangerous uses, and making models robust to spurious signals.

  • Jailbreak attacks and defenses, prompt injection, content-moderation guardrails
  • Robust and generalizable machine learning

Speaking & Collaboration

I give talks for academic, industry, and policy audiences, and I welcome collaborations. Recent hosts include NIH, the NAIRR Pilot, Stanford, Tsinghua University, Capital One, and EMBL-EBI.

Talk topics

  • Agentic AI scientists for biomedical discovery: LLM agents that analyze genomic data at the scale of thousands of diseases
  • LLM jailbreaks and prompt-injection defense: how aligned models fail, and the security–fidelity tradeoff in defending them
  • Spatial gene regulation and disease genetics: machine learning for regulatory networks and Alzheimer's disease heterogeneity
  • Trustworthy machine learning: robustness, generalization, and spurious features (tutorials at KDD 2023 and IEEE ICHI 2023)

Collaboration directions

  • Agentic AI: new multi-agent architectures (reusable agent primitives, KV-cache communication between agents), efficient test-time scaling, agents that keep learning from their sources, and AI scientists that run discovery end to end
  • Trustworthy & secure AI: jailbreaks and red teaming across text, vision, and audio; prompt-injection defense for deployed agents; and how LLM-based search and generative engines rank and recommend content
  • Computational biology: virtual bioinformaticians that analyze thousands of diseases at once, gene regulation from spatial and single-cell data, and shared genetic architecture across Alzheimer's disease, autism, and other complex diseases
  • Tools for real-world impact: platforms that put these methods in users' hands, such as Heelo, TopCited AI, and MyDataPilot

To invite a talk or discuss a collaboration, email haohanw at illinois.edu.

About Me

Haohan Wang is an Assistant Professor in the School of Information Sciences at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, where he leads the DREAM Lab. He is also an affiliated faculty member of the Siebel School of Computing and Data Science, the Carl R. Woese Institute for Genomic Biology, the Beckman Institute for Advanced Science and Technology, the Interdisciplinary Health Sciences Institute, and the National Center for Supercomputing Applications.

His research develops AI methods for scientific discovery. His group builds agentic AI systems that automate complex genomic data analysis, computational methods that recover gene regulatory structure from spatial and single-cell transcriptomics and explain the genetic basis of complex diseases such as Alzheimer's disease, and techniques that make large language models robust and secure against jailbreaks and prompt injection.

Wang received his Ph.D. (2021) and M.S. (2015) from the Language Technologies Institute at Carnegie Mellon University, where he worked with Professor Eric Xing, and his B.S. in computer science from Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications. Before joining Illinois in 2022, he was a postdoctoral researcher in the Machine Learning Department at CMU. In 2019, the Broad Institute of MIT and Harvard recognized him as one of the Next Generation in Biomedicine for his work on handling confounding factors with deep learning.

The Chinese spelling of his name is 汪浩瀚.
